# Specialists

**Specialists** are special individuals attached to settlements or formations who provide bonuses.

- Must be **unlocked** via technology developments and/or buildings.
- Can be **trained** (at the expense of resources and/or research points) or **developed over
  time**.
- Cost an **upkeep** amount.
- Provide benefits to the settlement or formation they are attached to.
- Can be **captured or killed** via conquest of a settlement, or the capture/kill of a formation.
- Can be **targeted by assassins** within a formation.

On a settlement, a specialist is displayed as an icon with `x / y`, where `x` is the number
currently existing and `y` is the total who can be hired. When the full amount is hired, just show
`x`.

Specialists progress along a **class** path (e.g. Blacksmith → Journeyman → Mastersmith). They gain
experience in their roles and become more effective over time — which makes experienced specialists
attractive targets for espionage and assassination. See
[departments-advisors.md](departments-advisors.md) for how specialists fill Department positions,
and [manufacturing-chains.md](manufacturing-chains.md#example-blacksmith) for a worked progression.

## Military commanders

Military commanders are a type of specialist, paid for in **research points**. The higher the rank,
the longer it takes (or the more research points it costs). For example, you might generate 3,000
research points per turn, but the military academy may only spend 100 per turn; a general may take
500.

- Combat experience can be **converted** toward military commanders. An entire unit can be sent "to
  the academy" via a button; a percentage then emerge at various ranks. Higher combat experience
  raises the likelihood of higher ranks. This doesn't necessarily *complete* their training, but
  greatly reduces it.
- The commander a formation has determines **how many units** it can contain and **how many
  specialists** can be assigned to it. (A unit, therefore, does not itself have command experience.)

| Rank | Max # of men | # of specialists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lieutenant | 44 | 1 |
| Captain | 200 | 3 |
| Major | 1,000 | 5 |
| Colonel | 3,200 | 10 |
| Major General | 10,000–16,000 | 12 |
| General | | 15 |

> Open idea: NCO ranks (Corporal, Sergeant, Master Sergeant) could increase the number of men per
> unit.
